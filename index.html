Dr. Divyesh Mistry

Dr. Divyesh Mistry is a computational materials scientist. His research centers on physics-informed multiscale descriptions of plasticity, defect evolution, and fracture in advanced structural materials, including nickel-based superalloys and tungsten alloys. He integrates molecular dynamics, discrete dislocation dynamics, and continuum mechanics to identify mechanistic links between defect-scale interactions and macroscopic constitutive responses.

Dr. Mistry completed his Ph.D. in Aerospace Engineering from the Indian Institute of Technology Bombay, where he was awarded the prestigious MHRD Teaching Assistantship. He holds an M.Tech. in Mechanical Engineering from the Indian Institute of Technology Kharagpur, specializing in Mechanical System Design.

Professional Summary

Computational Materials Scientist with over 5 years of experience specializing in atomistic simulations and multiscale modeling of deformation mechanisms in advanced structural materials. Expert in deploying large-scale molecular dynamics (MD) workflows using LAMMPS on high-performance computing (HPC) platforms to characterize defect evolution, dislocation dynamics, and fracture under extreme thermo-mechanical loading conditions. Highly proficient in utilizing Python for scientific computing, high-volume simulation data analysis, and predictive model development. Proven track record of conducting microstructure-sensitive modeling of high-temperature alloys and refractory metals, directly transferable to predicting material behavior in nuclear and reactor environments.

Developed a multiscale material model to study dislocation behavior, stress distribution, and strengthening mechanisms around PPBs and γ/γ' phases. Addressed challenges in modeling complex interactions between PPBs and dislocation dynamics in Ni-based superalloys

Dislocation Avalanche Mechanisms in Metallic Materials

Dislocation Dynamics (DDD) framework, implemented in FORTRAN, to explore the intricate mechanisms of dislocation avalanche dynamics and their influence on material behavior. Post-processing and data analysis are conducted using C++ and Python for visualization and data manipulation capabilities.
