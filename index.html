Research profile

From local mechanisms
to material response.

My research examines how defects, microstructure, and interfaces govern the deformation and failure of structural materials.

I use molecular dynamics (MD) to study nickel-based superalloys, tungsten alloys, and polymeric adhesives, and discrete dislocation dynamics (DDD) to investigate plasticity in metals. My doctoral research at IIT Bombay connected atomistically derived material properties with mesoscale models of precipitate strengthening and prior particle boundary effects.

At the University of Seville, I study cured epoxy adhesives within a research programme on steel/adhesive interfaces and aqueous degradation. Previously, as a Postdoctoral Research Fellow at Merrimack College, I investigated crack-tip plasticity in tungsten and tungsten-rhenium (W–Re) alloys.

Current research · University of Seville

The molecular foundations
of durable adhesive joints.

How do polymer chemistry and network structure influence the mechanical response of adhesives?

I construct and analyse cross-linked epoxy systems to connect resin-hardener composition, curing conversion, and network topology with deformation and energy response. The wider programme investigates steel/adhesive interfacial delamination under aqueous corrosion.

The aim is to provide molecular-scale input for models of environmental damage and joint lifetime.

ONR Global · Funded research

Multiscale modeling of interfacial delamination of steel/adhesive interfaces by aqueous corrosion
