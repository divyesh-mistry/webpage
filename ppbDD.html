Prior Particle Boundaries in Ni-based Superalloys

A polycrystalline discrete dislocation dynamics investigation of how PPB size, number, and spatial arrangement govern dislocation pinning, strain hardening, and residual-stress retention.

View publication All publications

Mechanistic focus

Prior particle boundaries formed during powder-metallurgy processing can remain as strong internal barriers after consolidation. The study represents these interfaces within a realistic polycrystalline DDD framework and examines their influence during loading, unloading, and stress-free annealing.

The simulations isolate a consistent mechanism: PPBs restrict transmission, promote localized pile-ups and back stresses, and preserve microstructural heterogeneity after unloading. Their effect depends not only on how many boundaries are present, but also on whether they form compact or spatially dispersed clusters.

Principal findings

Strain hardening

Larger and more distributed PPB populations increase dislocation immobilization and strengthen the hardening response.

Residual stress

PPB-adjacent regions retain stronger compressive residual stresses after unloading and subsequent annealing.

Spatial organization

Cluster topology changes the distribution of plastic activity, demonstrating that PPB arrangement matters alongside boundary count.

Microstructure-resolved evidence

Complementary field maps show how PPB-constrained deformation persists beyond the global stress--strain response and remains spatially tied to the surrounding grain structure.
