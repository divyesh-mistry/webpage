Research question & contribution

What happens when
internal boundaries remain?

Prior particle boundaries (PPBs) formed during powder-metallurgy processing can persist after consolidation and change the way a superalloy deforms.

I developed and applied a two-dimensional polycrystalline discrete dislocation dynamics (DDD) framework to study PPB clusters during loading, unloading, and annealing. The model represents realistic grain morphologies and tracks dislocation activity, hardening, geometrically necessary dislocation (GND) density, and residual stress.

By varying PPB cluster size, number, and spatial distribution, the study connects local dislocation blocking and back stresses with the macroscopic mechanical response.

Model scope. PPBs are represented as non-transmissive interfaces, corresponding to a limiting case of strong dislocation blocking.

Principal findings

Local barriers.
Collective consequences.

01 · Dislocation activity

Pile-ups and back stresses

Non-transmissive PPB clusters constrain dislocation motion and promote local pile-ups, changing the spatial organisation of plasticity.

02 · Mechanical response

Microstructure-dependent hardening

Larger and more densely distributed PPB clusters increase dislocation pinning and hardening within the studied configurations.

03 · Stress recovery

Retained residual fields

Regions adjacent to PPBs retain heterogeneous compressive stresses after unloading, with relaxation dependent on PPB density.
